# Creative direction

The drawing earns its place by explaining something. A figure pressing a button is useful if a result visibly follows; repeating a button in every scene becomes a mannerism. Use carrying, sheltering, sorting, waiting, comparing, stacking, revealing or checking when they fit the idea.

## Script

Open with the actual question or a specific surprise, not "Did you know?" or an introduction to the channel. Give viewers the comparison or promise early. Explain the rule in everyday English before giving examples. The examples should show the rule working. End by giving the viewer something to notice or do; a generic follow/subscribe line is optional, not automatic.

For example, the approved Quran Reel opens with the difference between two expressions, says that their English renderings can sound almost identical, attributes the observation, then shows two examples on each side. The curiosity comes from what follows each question. This structure can transfer to another subject; its religious claims cannot.

The hook is visual as well as spoken. In the Europe example, the map is already open at frame zero; the viewer should not wait for an unrolling routine to discover the topic. Keep later actions when they explain something, such as walking the connected mainland or pencilling a conventional boundary. A quiet opening pose can be the right choice.

A closing discussion question can invite a response when it fits the brief: “Would you call Europe a continent—or part of Eurasia?” Keep it connected to the explanation. It is optional; do not add a new geographic or religious claim solely for engagement.

One sentence per thought. Avoid jargon, breathless fragments, forced suspense and overclaiming. Estimate timing at a realistic speaking pace; names, Arabic quotations and pauses take longer. If a requested 30-second format can't hold four examples, discuss the tradeoff before recording. An already approved longer script should remain intact unless the user requests an edit.

## Layout

The starter uses headings at y=350/442, optional Arabic around y=610/685, a scene centered at (510,1150) and enlarged 2.65×, and captions around y=1350/1408. Compose around these spaces. Native scene coordinates are roughly x=-170..170 and y=-175..0; foot level is y=0. Native 9px labels become about 24px in the video: use them as secondary details, not the only place a key fact appears.

Keep essential labels off visually busy details and clear of book edges. The Europe example moves EURASIA below both books and enlarges it. A label can pass a safe-area check and still be unreadable. Map labels should sit over the area they name rather than at the far edge; use map coordinates when possible. For a held label, reposition the holder and recheck the grip too.

Keep captions concise, normally one or two lines. If words don't fit, rewrite/split the caption or adapt the layout. Do not shrink the whole scene to preserve a decorative container. Inspect Arabic vowel marks as well as Latin letters. The safe overlay is a checking aid and must stay out of the export.

The included safe rectangle is the accepted organic-Reel working area, not official or permanent platform guidance. Check the actual publishing destination when platform-specific guarantees matter; expanded captions and UI can cover more of the image.

## Purposeful movement

Before coding, write a small shot list with **verb → object → visible result**. Check whether the list repeats the same action with different labels. If it does, return to the original examples: lifting/stacking, passing a note, catching/tipping, peeling, stamping, cranking, hanging and filming each communicate a different relationship. A person actually doing a job reads more clearly than a person gesturing at a diagram.

For companions, give both characters a reason to exist: one asks or hands something over, the other thinks, works, receives or returns it. Use a visible delay and a response. Keep their work areas distinct and coordinate the transfer. Do not add a robot to religious teaching in a role that could imply it represents Allah or a prophet.

Useful small motions come from the action: a bag sways during a carry, paper tilts in a grip, a thrown object rotates, a bucket settles on impact, a held net follows the envelope, and a cup can steam while a character waits. They are possibilities, not facts about every original scene. A backpack, sipping action, watering action or two-person shared load must be deliberately implemented and checked; don't claim an example already contains it when it doesn't.

Draw fixed props before posing the figure. Pick the exact contact point, place the figure within reach, then use `lp(worldTarget, figureX, figureY, sx)`. For a held umbrella or tool, anchor the drawing to `J.w.handF` or use `held`. Idle arms hang at the sides. A hand moving toward a target briefly passes through empty space; a held gesture must finish on a visible target.

Use a small anticipation, action, visible result, and reading hold. Let a question remain unanswered until its answer is spoken. The example Quran Reel separates question and answer scene variants so the star/calendar doesn't reveal the answer early. After the payoff, hold it through the cut rather than showing the reusable scene rewind.

Recurring companions should be easy to distinguish by a consistent silhouette, accessory or color. The Europe example gives the blue companion the original runtime’s curved-crown, brimmed hat (`hat: true`); it follows the head automatically. Inspect existing accessory geometry before drawing a replacement. A small flat rectangle may technically attach correctly but read poorly as a hat. Character styling is an option, not a requirement to put the same hat on every production.

Vary the metaphor with the thought, not every two seconds. Use color to distinguish ideas. Use head turns, breathing and small changes of posture when no hand action is needed. Leave unnecessary cheering and pointing out.

## Review

Look at native motion sheets and the final vertical sheet. The native view catches anatomy; the vertical view catches small text, empty areas, captions competing with props and reveal timing. Watch with narration: a mechanically clean scene can still be dull, misleading or out of sync. Use the examples as comparisons, not proof that new scenes work.
