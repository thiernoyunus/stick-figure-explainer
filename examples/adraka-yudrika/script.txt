What's the difference between wa mā adrāka and wa mā yudrīka in the Quran?

In English, they're translated almost identically: "What could make you know?"

But the scholar Sufyan ibn 'Uyaynah pointed out a difference:
Whenever Allah says wa mā adrāka, He gives an explanation. With wa mā yudrīka, that specific answer isn't given.

Listen to these examples.

وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا لَيْلَةُ الْقَدْرِ
"What can make you know what the Night of Decree is?"

The next verse explains:
لَيْلَةُ الْقَدْرِ خَيْرٌ مِنْ أَلْفِ شَهْرٍ
"The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months."

And again:
وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا الطَّارِقُ
"What can make you know what the night visitor is?"

النَّجْمُ الثَّاقِبُ
"The piercing star."

Both times, an explanation follows.
Now compare wa mā yudrīka:

وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ لَعَلَّ السَّاعَةَ تَكُونُ قَرِيبًا
"What would make you know? Perhaps the Hour is near."

We're told it may be near. We aren't told when.

And:
وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ لَعَلَّهُ يَزَّكَّىٰ
"What would make you know? Perhaps he might be purified."

A possibility is mentioned. His outcome isn't confirmed there.

So next time you hear these phrases, listen closely:
Wa mā adrāka — an explanation follows.
Wa mā yudrīka — the specific answer isn't revealed.

Two expressions that can sound similar in English — but watch how differently the Quran uses them.
