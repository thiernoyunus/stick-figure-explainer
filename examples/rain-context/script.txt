Two words in the Quran. Both translated as rain. So, what's the difference?

Matar means rain. In the Quran, it often appears in harmful contexts.

In seven, eighty-four, it describes a destructive shower, not normal rainfall.

Ghayth highlights welcome rain, bringing relief.

In forty-two, twenty-eight, Allah sends rain after people lose hope, and spreads His mercy.

But matar doesn't always mean punishment. Four, one-oh-two uses it for rain causing hardship.

The takeaway? Matar: often harmful contexts. Ghayth: rain as relief. Always read the context.
