Why is Europe considered a separate continent when there's no ocean separating it from Asia?

Look at a map. From Portugal to China, the mainland is connected. No ocean separates Europe from Asia.

That landmass has a name: Eurasia.

If you define a continent as a major, separate landmass, Europe doesn't qualify on its own.

So where did the division come from?

Ancient Greek thinkers distinguished Europe from Asia, including their Persian rivals. The separation became tied to identity and politics—not just the shape of the land.

Later, European geographers chose the Ural Mountains as a boundary. But drawing a line across a landmass doesn't create another one.

And the seven-continent system isn't taught everywhere. Russian and Japanese school materials describe Eurasia as one continent, with Europe and Asia treated as regions within it.

Europe's separate-continent status comes from a historical division—not a separate piece of the Earth.
