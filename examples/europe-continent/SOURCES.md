# Europe: one landmass, historical divisions

The approved script is in `script.txt`, with the final approved introduction. It argues from the **separate-major-landmass definition**, explicitly stated in the narration. It does not claim there is one universally binding continent definition. Europe remains a continent in the conventional seven-continent scheme. No claim about a single racial motive is included.

- [UCLA World History for Us All — Eurasia](https://whfua.history.ucla.edu/getting_started.php): Europe/Asia are conjoined, the separate-landmass definition does not apply to either alone; eighteenth-century European geographers designated the Urals as their boundary. The curriculum treats Europe as a subcontinent of Eurasia or Afroeurasia.
- [National Geographic — Continent](https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/Continent/): cultural/historical distinctions help explain dividing Eurasia into Europe and Asia; the physical land remains connected.
- [Andrew Glencross, The Politics of European Integration, chapter 1, pp.14–15](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781405193955.excerpt.pdf): ancient Greek distinctions from Persian rivals, and the development of Europe as an idea of geographical/cultural/political separateness. The script summarizes a historical development, not a claim that all modern Europeans share one intention.
- [Russian grade-seven geography lesson, Rostelecom Lyceum](https://lc.rt.ru/classbook/geografiya-7-klass/materiki-evraziya-604/3654): Europe and Asia are parts of the world forming the landmass/continent Eurasia.
- [Japanese geography teaching material, Education Network](https://www.edu-network.jp/download/%EF%BD%BB%EF%BE%9D%EF%BE%8C%EF%BE%9F%EF%BE%99%2802667_21%29.pdf): six continents include Eurasia; Europe and Asia are separate regions. These are actual teaching examples, not a claim about every classroom.

Sources reviewed 2026-11-18. Landmasses and tectonic plates are different concepts. A plate boundary is not used as the definition in this video. Africa is also connected to Eurasia via the Suez isthmus; the map retains surrounding African land rather than inventing an oceanic gap.

## Map

Coastlines use the public-domain [Natural Earth 1:110m land dataset](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/110m-physical-vectors/110m-land/), obtained from its [maintainer's GeoJSON repository](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/master/geojson/ne_110m_land.geojson). Original source data is retained in `assets/natural-earth-land.geojson`. [Natural Earth terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/) allow modification and redistribution.

The map is a cropped teaching view of Eurasia and nearby land, using a simple longitude/latitude projection. Insets and floor-map perspective are explanatory drawings, not navigation aids. Any dashed Europe/Asia boundary is schematic and represents one conventional route; it is not a coastline, state border, universally agreed frontier, or precise survey line. Portugal and China markers are representative positions.

## Production

Generic modern explorers/cartographers and teaching props. The ancient-history shot uses documents and regional labels rather than portraying particular historical people. White background; roomy working text-safe rectangle x=60–960, y=220–1500. Guides are excluded from the final video. Voice recording and actual generation metadata are retained locally. No API key is stored in the project. The video is not published automatically.
